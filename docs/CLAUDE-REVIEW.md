# Blackout — Game Spec

**Tagline:** *Get rich. Keep the lights on. Pick one.*
**Format:** Semi-competitive, 3–6 players, ~10 minutes. Phones are private control panels; a laptop/TV is the shared grid screen.
**Status:** design proposal from Claude for Codex to build against. Disagreements go in issue #1 (`[CODEX]`/`[CLAUDE]` protocol). Codex owns all implementation decisions.

**Provenance note:** items marked *design decision* are our choices, not verified mission requirements (the mission page is login-gated).

---

## 1. The one-sentence pitch

Everyone runs a power plant on one shared grid and races to make the most money, but squeezing the grid for profit pushes the whole city toward a blackout that sinks everyone.

## 2. What the player should feel

- **Round 2:** "If I hold back a bit, prices go up…"
- **Round 5 (evening peak):** The frequency needle shakes into the red. Someone begs: "Gas, I need 20 more, I'll pay you!"
- **End:** The **Ledger reveal** shows who quietly withheld power while promising not to. Laughter, outrage, instant rematch.

## 3. Plants

Each player gets one plant (random, or chosen in lobby; no duplicates until all types are taken).

| Plant | Capacity (MW) | Fuel cost ($/MWh) | Ramp per round | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nuclear | 120 | 10 | ±20 MW | Cheap, steady, painfully slow |
| Coal | 100 | 30 | ±30 MW | 10% breakdown chance each round above 90% output |
| Gas | 80 | 60 | Any level | Fast, expensive — the grid's emergency lever |
| Solar | 90 | 0 | Set by sun (see demand table) | Free; zero at night; clouds cut it |
| Wind | 80 | 0 | Set by weather card | Free; unpredictable |
| Battery | 60 MW / 120 MWh store | 0 (charges by buying cheap power) | Instant | Can **charge** in cheap rounds and **discharge** at peak |

**All numbers are tuning values.** Codex: put them in one config file so we can rebalance after playtests.

## 4. The day (8 rounds)

**Demand scales to the plants actually in the room**, so any mix of players is balanced:

```
peak_demand = (total capacity available at 8 pm, i.e. excluding solar, battery at full store) ÷ 1.15
round_demand = peak_demand × demand_fraction[round]
```

The grid can meet peak demand with ~15% to spare, so **one player withholding or one breakdown is enough to cause trouble.** That is the point.

| Round | Time | Demand (% of peak) | Solar available | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 am | 55% | 10% | Gentle start |
| 2 | 9 am | 65% | 60% | |
| 3 | 12 pm | 70% | 100% | Midday: solar floods in, prices dip |
| 4 | 3 pm | 80% | 80% | Heat builds |
| 5 | 6 pm | 95% | 20% | **Evening ramp — the danger zone** |
| 6 | 8 pm | 100% | 0% | **Peak** |
| 7 | 10 pm | 78% | 0% | |
| 8 | 12 am | 60% | 0% | Wind-down |

Example: Nuclear + Coal + Gas + Solar → 300 MW available at 8 pm → peak demand ≈ 261 MW.

This shape is the real **"duck curve"**: solar drives a midday dip in what other plants must supply, then a steep climb at sunset when solar vanishes and demand peaks. Weather and heat-wave events can push a round's demand up to +15%.

## 5. A round, step by step

1. **Forecast (shared screen, 5 s):** demand for this round, weather card, any public event.
2. **Private event cards (phones):** e.g., *"Your boiler is overheating: 30% chance of breakdown if you run above 90%."*
3. **Deal phase (30 s):** structured offers between players (section 7).
4. **Secret bids (20 s):** each player sets **output (MW)** within ramp limits and **asking price ($/MWh)**. Battery chooses charge / hold / discharge.
5. **Market clears (animated, 4 s):** the grid accepts the **cheapest bids first** until demand is met. **Every accepted plant is paid the clearing price** = the highest accepted bid. (This is how real wholesale electricity markets set prices.) Profit = (clearing price − fuel cost) × MW dispatched.
6. **Breakdowns roll.** A plant that breaks delivers 0 this round → shortfall.
7. **Frequency updates** (section 6). Shared screen shows the needle and the city's lights.

## 6. Grid frequency (the drama engine)

Shortfall = demand − delivered supply (after breakdowns).

```
frequency = 60.0 − 2.0 × (shortfall / demand)      [clamped to 57.0–60.2]
```

| Zone | Frequency | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Green | ≥ 59.8 Hz | Normal |
| Yellow | 59.3 – 59.8 Hz | Warning siren; everyone's revenue −10% this round |
| Orange | 58.8 – 59.3 Hz | **Rolling blackouts:** districts go dark on the city map; revenue −40% for everyone |
| Red | < 58.8 Hz, or Orange two rounds in a row | **Cascade:** the city goes dark district by district; the game ends immediately |

Real grids run at 60 Hz in North America, and automatic load shedding is triggered when frequency falls far enough below it. **These zone values are game-tuned for play, not real operating thresholds**; Claude will check the debrief wording so it doesn't overstate realism.

## 7. Deals (structured, so bots can play)

Offered during the deal phase from a fixed menu; the other player accepts or declines:
- **Reserve pact:** "I'll keep X MW available if you do."
- **Cover me:** "If my plant breaks, you supply X MW; I pay you $Y now."
- **Price truce:** "We both bid ≤ $Z this round."
- **Pay to run:** "I'll pay you $Y to add X MW."

Deals are **not enforced.** Breaking one is legal. But every deal and whether it was honored is recorded for the **Ledger** (section 9).

## 8. Scoring and anti-spite

- **Winner:** most money at the end.
- **Reliability bonus:** grid finishes the day with zero Orange/Red rounds → **everyone** gets +$500. Real incentive to cooperate.
- **Regulator investigation:** if the grid **cascades**, each player's *withheld capacity* (available MW they didn't offer, summed over the last 3 rounds) is compared. The top withholder(s) are fined 50% of their money; a "FINED" stamp lands on their card. A losing player who crashes the grid on purpose gets punished, not rewarded.
- **Price cap:** bids above $500/MWh are rejected (prevents degenerate infinite-price strategies).

## 9. The Ledger reveal (end screen)

The signature moment. After the final round, animate:
1. Frequency through the day as a line, with blackout bands shaded.
2. Each player's **promised vs actual** for every deal ("Promised 20 MW reserve → delivered 0").
3. Awards with personality: **The Hero** (most MW delivered in danger rounds), **The Profiteer** (highest average price), **The Snake** (most broken deals), **The Saint** (honored every deal).
4. One "real world" card: *"In 2000–01, energy traders including Enron withheld power to push up prices, and California suffered rolling blackouts."* (Claude will finalize and source all real-world copy.)
5. Rematch button.

## 10. Bots (*design decision*: enables a solo judge to experience the social dilemma)

Three personalities, deterministic rules (seeded randomness only):
- **Cooperative:** bids near fuel cost, offers full available capacity, honors all deals, proposes reserve pacts when frequency forecast is tight.
- **Greedy:** withholds 15–30% of capacity when demand > 90% of capacity; bids high; honors deals only when the grid is in Yellow or worse.
- **Snake:** accepts every deal, breaks ~60% of them; withholds aggressively near peak.

A solo judge plays one plant against three bots (one of each) and sees the social dilemma in a single game.

## 11. Shared screen layout

- City skyline across the top; districts light/dim with frequency.
- Big frequency gauge center; demand vs supply bar beside it.
- Plants as nodes around the city, each with its owner's color and current output (not price — prices are secret until the market clears).
- Event ticker along the bottom.
- Hum sound whose pitch tracks frequency.

## 12. Phone screen layout

- Your plant card: type, capacity, ramp limit, fuel cost, status.
- Two big sliders: **Output** and **Price**. A live estimate: "If the price clears at $X, you earn $Y."
- Deal inbox with accept/decline.
- Private event card banner.

## 13. First milestone scope (Codex's "one playable market round" in issue #1)

Proposed: 3 players (bots allowed), 3 plant types (Nuclear, Gas, Solar), 3 rounds, secret bids, market clearing, frequency gauge with zones, one breakdown event. No deals, no Ledger, no sound.

## 14. Out of scope

Transmission lines/geography, real-time continuous play, accounts, persistence. Live AI at runtime is excluded as a *design decision*, not a contest rule.

[CLAUDE] Rules challenge (task 2). No SHA; this reviews the spec.

**Verdict:** the concept holds, but as written **withholding is a dominant strategy and blackouts almost never happen.** Fixes below, in priority order.

**1. Frequency formula is far too forgiving (High).**
`f = 60 − 2 × (shortfall/demand)` means Orange (< 59.3) needs a **35% shortfall** and Red needs **60%**. That will basically never happen, so there's no drama.
**Fix:** use `f = 60 − 6 × (shortfall/demand)`. Yellow then starts at ~3% short, Orange at ~12%, Red at 20%. One withholder or one breakdown at peak now matters, which is the whole game.

**2. Scarcity pricing makes withholding dominant (High).**
When supply < demand, the clearing price is undefined, and the natural rule (pay the cap, $500) gives about 8× a normal price. Even after Orange's −40% revenue cut, withholding pays.
**Fix (also realistic):** under stress the grid operator takes over.
- **Yellow:** clearing price capped at 2.5× the median fuel cost.
- **Orange:** **emergency dispatch.** All offered capacity is paid cost + 10%. Nobody profits from a crisis.

Profit then lives in Green and early Yellow, which is where the bluffing should happen.

**3. "Withheld capacity" can be gamed (High).**
Bidding $499 for full capacity isn't counted as withholding under the current definition. That's *economic* withholding.
**Fix:** for fines, count withheld capacity as (available MW not offered) **plus** (MW offered above 3× that plant's fuel cost).

**4. Solar and Wind are boring after sunset (Medium).**
Solar sits idle for 3 of 8 rounds with no decisions; Wind has none beyond price.
**Fix:**
- **Solar** becomes **Solar + Storage** (90 MW panels + 30 MW / 60 MWh battery): charge at midday, sell into the evening peak. Gives it the best arc in the game.
- **Wind** gets a private forecast card one round ahead and can sell **"firm" promises** that it is fined for missing.

**5. Nuclear has thin decisions (Low).**
Its only choice is price, under a slow ramp.
**Fix:** a mid-game **"refuel outage" decision.** Take 0 MW for one round now for +20% output later, or risk a forced outage at peak (25%). That's a real planning dilemma.

**6. End-game defection (Low, acceptable).**
Round 8 has no future consequences, so everyone defects. The reliability bonus already counters this. Keep it, and announce "final round" so it feels deliberate.

**7. Battery buying (clarify).**
Charging = the battery buys MW at the clearing price, which adds to that round's demand. State that in the rules UI so the market result stays explainable.

**8. Sabotage check after fixes.**
A trailing player who crashes the grid is fined and gains nothing, because Orange pays cost + 10%. The leader can't profit from a crash either. I see no remaining incentive to sabotage on purpose. Please confirm with a quick seeded simulation once the market logic exists (greedy vs. cooperative bots over 100 games). Withholding should win sometimes, not always.
